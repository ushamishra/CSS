It is the world's seventh-largest country by area and the largest by population.[21] Bounded by the Indian Ocean on the south, the Arabian Sea on the southwest, and the Bay of Bengal on the southeast, it shares land borders with Pakistan to the west;[k] China, Nepal, and Bhutan to the north; and Bangladesh and Myanmar to the east. In the Indian Ocean, India is near Sri Lanka and the Maldives.[l] Modern humans arrived on the Indian subcontinent from Africa by 55,000 years ago.[23][24][25] Their long, isolated occupation as hunter-gatherers made the region highly diverse.[26] Settled life emerged on the western margins of the Indus basin 9,000 years ago, evolving into the Indus Valley Civilisation by the third millennium BCE.[27] By 1200 BCE, an archaic form of Sanskrit had diffused into India from the northwest,[28][29][30] its hymns recording the early dawnings of Hinduism.[31] India's pre-existing Dravidian languages were supplanted in the northern regions.[32] By 400 BCE, social differentiation by caste had consolidated within Hinduism.[33] Contemporarily, Buddhism and Jainism had arisen, proclaiming social orders unlinked to heredity.[34] The emergence of the loose-knit Maurya and Gupta Empires,[35] gave rise to wide-ranging cultural and scientific creativity,[36] but their eras also witnessed a steady erosion of women's rights,[37] and, by the end, the rigid institutionalisation of untouchability.[m][39] Concurrently accross the first millennium CE, the Middle kingdoms of South India transmitted Dravidian language scripts and cultures to Southeast Asia.[40] Well before the millennium's end, Judaism, Christianity, Islam and Zoroastrianism had become established on India's southern and western coasts.[41]
